Technical proposal

Making the National Identity Card Verifiable

A signed QR code that binds the printed details and the cardholder’s photograph to the issuing authority. Deployable with the next print run. Verifiable offline, on any phone, by anyone.

Department of National Registration · Maldives Digital Service · Working prototype with a complete reference implementation

Frame the session: fifteen minutes, a live demonstration, and one decision at the end.

Today, verifying an identity card means looking at it.

That is a judgement about printing quality. It is not a verification of fact.

Pause here. Let the room sit with it. Everyone present has accepted a card on sight this week.

The problem

What the card cannot prove

  • That the name printed on it is the name the state issued
  • That the details have not been altered since issue
  • That the photograph is the one attached to that record
  • That the card is still in date, beyond reading a printed line

Banking, travel, employment, land, healthcare and voting all rest on this.

The problem
The problem

Forgery is not hypothetical

  • Courts note document forgery cases are increasing, and police have made them a priority
  • In an MVR 8 million recruitment and money laundering case, investigators recovered forged permits, passport photographs and identity cards of Maldivians from the principal’s devices
  • Twelve people charged over forged registry documents used to claim land under the Binveriya scheme

A forged card is rarely the goal. It is the instrument used to reach a permit, an account, a plot of land.

The problem

The photograph is the one element with no protection at all.

It is what connects a document to a human being — and nothing binds it to the data beside it.

This is the hinge of the whole pitch. Everything after this is the fix.

Section two

The proposal

The proposal

Add one QR code. Change nothing else.

It carries the six printed fields, a fingerprint of the photograph, and a signature from the issuing authority over all of it.

MV2#A123456#Aishath Nasheeda Ibrahim#F#1991-04-17#2036-08-27#Ma. Blue Heaven, Male, Maldives#290d5eb82deaf087#<signature>
Same card stock Same layout Same workflow No card recall
The proposal
The proposal

270 bytes of ink

A version 10 QR symbol in a 25 mm square: 0.44 mm per module. Comfortable for card printing, easy for a phone camera. Enhanced cards need version 11: 0.41 mm per module in the same square.

The proposal
The proposal

Four things become checkable

  • Authenticity — the signature verifies only against the authority’s key
  • Integrity — altering any field breaks the signature
  • Photo binding — swapping the photograph breaks the match
  • Validity — the expiry date is inside the signature, so it cannot be extended

Section three

How it works

Technology

Signatures: one key signs, everyone checks

The authority holds a private key and can produce signatures. Everyone else holds only the public key: they can check signatures, never create them.

BLS12-381
signature scheme, used in production at scale
96 B
signature size
48 B
public key, published freely

Publishing the public key nationally costs nothing and grants no ability to issue.

How it works
Technology

Binding the photograph

A cryptographic hash would reject a re-photographed picture. So the card carries a perceptual hash — 64 bits describing the image’s structure. Same face, nearly the same bits.

0
bits differ — genuine, scanned flat
2
bits differ — genuine, angled and noisy
34
bits differ — a different face
12
acceptance threshold

The margin is wide in both directions.

How it works
Technology

The photo is found by geometry, not by asking

QR decoders report the four corners of the symbol. The card is a rigid layout, so those corners determine exactly where the portrait must be. The verifier removes perspective and rotation mathematically.

That is why a hand-held photograph of a card lying at an angle still verifies.

Extended security

Extended security

MV3: matching the person, not just the photo

Standard (MV2) proves the card is genuine and the printed photo matches. Enhanced (MV3) adds a signed 128-bit face code and compares it with the person presenting the card — turning a document check into an identity check. Both tiers coexist; the version prefix tells the verifier which checks to run.

MV2
signature + photo hash
MV3
+ signed face code — live in the demo

Both tiers are in the demo build. Show card-enhanced.png on the verify page, then upload portrait-2.jpg (accepted) and portrait-1.jpg (rejected) in step 3.

Extended security
Extended security

How Enhanced verification works

  1. Same signature, expiry and printed-portrait checks as Standard.
  2. If the card is MV3, the verifier loads the face models (about 7 MB, once).
  3. The person is photographed; their face is reduced to the same 128-bit code on the device.
  4. Bits that differ from the signed code are counted — pass or fail at Enhanced assurance.
0–17
bits differ — same face, printed and re-photographed
34
threshold: 0.1% of different people accepted (LFW)
32–56
bits differ — different Maldivian sample faces

Nothing is uploaded. No liveness detection yet, so it belongs at staffed counters. A privacy impact assessment and measurement on Maldivian faces precede rollout.

Design choice

Why not a link QR?

A URL QR is easy to print and easy to fake. The forger points at a site that looks official and shows a green tick. Clone domains — verify-dnr.gov.mv, dnr.gov-mv.com, homographs — are routine phishing. Spotting the wrong domain means reading the URL bar under pressure.

A signed payload inverts the trust model: the card carries the evidence, the verifier pins one public key, and the maths pass or fail with no network call. A convincing website cannot forge a BLS12-381 signature.

If someone asks “why not just scan to verify.gov.mv?”, this is the slide.

Honesty

What this does not do

  • No offline revocation. A card reported stolen still verifies. That needs a connected status check.
  • Standard cards confirm the picture, not the person. Enhanced cards add a face check, but without liveness detection — staffed counters only.
  • The face model is not tuned for Maldivian faces. Two different sample women land inside the threshold. It must be measured on real enrolment photos, and likely replaced, before anyone relies on it.
  • Key compromise is catastrophic. The signing key must live in an HSM under split control.
  • Nothing is encrypted. A scanner reads what the card already shows — but machine-readable is not the same as visible.

Say this slide out loud rather than skipping it. Advisors in the room will already be thinking it, and volunteering it buys credibility for everything else.

Delivery

Rollout and cost

Phases

  • Pilot, security review, print and camera trials — 2–3 months
  • HSM and issuance integration — 3–4 months
  • Verifier release, in parallel
  • Cards gain the QR through normal reissue

Cost profile

  • Zero per card — the QR is ink
  • One HSM — the only significant capital item
  • Zero reader hardware, at every checkpoint, ever
  • No card replacement programme
Delivery
Coexistence

Layers, not competitors

The chip serves payments, fingerprint verification and online authentication wherever a reader exists. The signed QR serves universal offline verification, on the same card, for everyone else.

Printing the QR on the Smart ID card costs nothing — and means the card still proves something when the reader is absent, broken or unpowered.

Coexistence
The ask

Authorise a time-boxed pilot

  • Approve a pilot with DNR and Maldives Digital Service
  • Commission an independent security review
  • Authorise HSM procurement and a documented key ceremony
  • Agree which fields belong in a machine-readable payload
  • Direct that the Smart ID specification reserve space for a printed signed QR

The alternative is leaving the card unverifiable for another two years.
